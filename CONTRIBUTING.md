# Contributing to "Why Is It Called That?" 🤝

Thank you for your interest in helping non-native English speakers understand software engineering terminology! Your contributions make this resource more valuable for developers worldwide.

## 🎯 What We're Looking For

### Term Suggestions
- **Common software engineering terms** that programmers encounter regularly
- Terms with **interesting etymology** or origin stories
- Concepts that are **often misunderstood** by non-native speakers
- Technical terms where **understanding the origin helps with usage**

### Quality Standards
- **Accurate etymology** with reliable sources when possible
- **Clear explanations** accessible to non-native speakers
- **Practical examples** showing real-world usage
- **Cultural sensitivity** and inclusive language

## 📝 How to Contribute

### Adding a New Term

1. **Check if it exists**: Search existing files to avoid duplicates
2. **Research the etymology**: Find reliable sources for the origin
3. **Follow the template** below for consistency
4. **Add to multiple files**: Update `terms.md`, `categories.md`, and `quick-reference.md`

### Term Template
```markdown
### [Term Name]
**Origin**: [Etymology with source/context]

**Meaning**: [Clear definition in simple language]

**Why this makes sense**: [Explain the connection between origin and usage]

**Example**: [Practical example showing usage in context]
```

### Example Entry
```markdown
### Spam
**Origin**: From the Monty Python sketch where "Spam" (canned meat) was repeated annoyingly

**Meaning**: Unwanted or repetitive messages, usually in email

**Why this makes sense**: Like the annoying repetition of "Spam" in the comedy sketch, spam emails are annoyingly repetitive and unwanted.

**Example**: "I got 20 spam emails trying to sell me fake watches."
```

## 🔍 Research Guidelines

### Reliable Sources
- **Etymological dictionaries** (Oxford, Merriam-Webster)
- **Technical documentation** from original creators
- **Academic papers** about computing history
- **Reputable technology history books**
- **Official organization websites** (IEEE, ACM)

### Avoid
- Wikipedia as the only source (use it for leads, then verify)
- Urban legends or unverified stories
- Personal blogs without citations
- Speculation without evidence

## 🌍 Language Considerations

### Writing for Non-Native Speakers
- **Use simple, clear language**
- **Avoid idioms** and cultural references
- **Define technical terms** when necessary
- **Provide phonetic pronunciation** for difficult words
- **Use concrete examples** rather than abstract concepts

### Example of Good vs Poor Explanations

❌ **Poor**: "Cache comes from the French word meaning to hide, and it's basically like a secret stash where you keep your most precious data treasures for lightning-fast access."

✅ **Good**: "Cache comes from French 'cacher' meaning 'to hide'. It stores frequently used data in a quickly accessible location, like keeping your most-used tools in an easy-to-reach drawer."

## 🎨 Style Guidelines

### Formatting
- Use **bold** for emphasis on key concepts
- Use `code formatting` for technical terms in examples
- Use > blockquotes for important notes
- Keep consistent emoji usage (✅ ❌ 🎯 💡)

### Tone
- **Friendly and encouraging**
- **Educational, not condescending**
- **Enthusiastic about learning**
- **Respectful of different backgrounds**

## 🚀 Getting Started

### Quick Contribution
1. **Pick a term** from software engineering
2. **Research its origin** using reliable sources
3. **Write the entry** following our template
4. **Open a pull request** with your addition

### Larger Contributions
- **Add an entire category** of related terms
- **Improve existing explanations** with better examples
- **Add pronunciation guides** for difficult terms
- **Create visual aids** or diagrams
- **Translate content** to other languages

## 🔄 Review Process

### What We Look For
- **Accuracy** of etymology and definitions
- **Clarity** for non-native speakers
- **Completeness** of the entry
- **Consistency** with existing style
- **Usefulness** to the target audience

### Common Feedback
- "Can you simplify this explanation?"
- "Do you have a source for this etymology?"
- "Could you add a more practical example?"
- "This might be too advanced for beginners"
